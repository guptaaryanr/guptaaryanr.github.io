Technip Energies

The dates overlap because the roles did.

2023 to 2025
and 2026

Technology Developer I · Applied Physics and Carbon Capture R&D

Technip Energies

  • This was most of my time at Technip. I built out the team’s MD/DFT capability across HPC and cloud systems, cut simulation wall time by roughly two thirds, and started carrying atomistic results into diffusion and CFD models instead of stopping at the molecular scale.
  • That also meant parameter sweeps, terabytes of image data, uncertainty analysis, surrogate models, and deciding which simulation or experiment was worth the next run.
  • I wrote 15 internal technical notes, maintained the code and data well enough for other people to reuse, mentored teammates, and increasingly ended up in the meetings where the technical result had to turn into a funding or engineering decision.
2025 to 2026

Americas AI Solutions Initiative · Cofounder and Technical Lead

Technip Energies · alongside engineering role

  • This started with a few of us trying to make internal AI work a little less random. It grew across 4 operating centers and 8 engineering functions. I handled much of the technical scoping, deciding what was worth building, what the first version actually needed to do, and which pieces could be reused instead of rebuilt for every demo.
  • The projects covered natural-language-to-SQL queries, P&ID analysis, vendor-document review, and bid tabulation. One bid tabulation pipeline reduced manual review effort by about 80%.
2025 to 2026

Specialist Engineer I · Instrumentation & Controls

Technip Energies

  • I spent my final year much closer to conventional EPC engineering. The work ran through P&IDs, PFDs, Cause & Effect diagrams, control narratives, vendor documents, and project specifications on hydrogen, carbon capture, and brownfield scopes.
  • Working with P&IDs, specifications, and change-controlled documents changed how I thought about engineering software. Traceability, document control, and QA have to be built into the workflow.
